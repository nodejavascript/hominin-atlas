Everyone alive is descended from a handful of people who walked out of Africa — and from the species they met on the way.

This is that story on one globe. Every hominin we have a fossil of, where it lived and when. Drag the timeline and watch species appear and disappear — and watch the places where two of them were in the same landscape at the same time, which is not where the word “replaced” usually points.

What this map will not do

  • It will not draw a population it cannot source. Of the localities here, most have no published population estimate at all. Those dots still have to have a size, so they have a nominal one — and the panel says so, in those words, when you open them.
  • It will not resolve a dispute. Where the literature disagrees, the date is marked contested and both readings are shown. The 65,000-year date for Madjedbebe, in Australia, is the clearest example on this map and is drawn as contested rather than as settled.
  • It will not show a coastline that was not there. The shaded shelf is drawn only for glacial periods in the last 1.2 million years. Further back the ice-age cycle was 41,000 years long and the record is not resolved enough to draw a coast from, so nothing is drawn.
  • It will not draw a route as a measured path. A route here is a line through dated sites in the order the archaeology reaches them. Nobody walked a straight line from Ethiopia to Java, and a line drawn 1.8 million years long is a summary of a process that took tens of thousands of years.
  • It will not pretend the record is complete. Whole species on this map are known from a single bone, a single jaw, or from nothing but a statistical signal in living genomes. The ghost population in West Africa has no fossil at all, and it is drawn as a ghost.

    Privacy

    This page collects nothing. There is no account, no form, no upload and no server-side storage: the globe is drawn in your browser from data that ships with the page, and the species filter and timeline position live in that tab's memory and are gone when the tab closes.

    The one thing that could leave your browser is anonymous measurement. Google Analytics loads only if you accept it. If you refuse, no request is made to Google, no cookie is set, and nothing is recorded — not "cookies disabled", but never loaded. Your answer is kept in this browser's local storage, not in a cookie, and the footer's Cookie settings button changes it at any time.

    The map, the coastline data and the three-dimensional library are all served from this domain. Following a citation link to a paper leaves this site and goes to the publisher, which has its own policy.
